# Retention Recommendations for IBM HR Attrition Project

## Executive summary

The analysis indicates that employee resignation risk is concentrated in a small group of individuals who share some combination of low satisfaction, overtime pressure, low compensation progression, and short tenure. Retention interventions should therefore be targeted, not generic.

## 1. Priority actions

1. Focus on employees with short tenure and low job satisfaction.
   - These individuals are the most likely to leave in the next cycle.
   - Managers should schedule check-ins at 30/60/90 days after probation.

2. Monitor overtime and workload pressure.
   - High `OverTime` exposure is a known risk factor in attrition analysis.
   - Require workload review when overtime becomes consistent.

3. Review compensation and progression.
   - Low salary growth and low stock option levels are common early-warning patterns.
   - Use pay calibration and promotion review for under-market roles.

4. Improve team-level job satisfaction and environment.
   - Low `EnvironmentSatisfaction` and `WorkLifeBalance` are strong retention signals.
   - Offer flexible work planning and manager coaching.

## 2. Role-specific actions

- Sales and customer-facing roles should receive early attrition risk review.
- Laboratory / technical / support roles should be monitored for workload and recognition gaps.
- Employees with multiple job switches or short tenure should receive targeted retention conversations.

## 3. Recommended KPI for HR

- Attrition rate by department and job role
- % high-risk employees with manager follow-up
- Satisfaction score trend by quarter
- Promotion and compensation review completion rate

## 4. Suggested implementing questions

- Which employees are in the top 10% predicted risk group?
- Which managers have the highest attrition concentration?
- Which roles need immediate intervention due to low job satisfaction?

This retention plan should be used alongside the predictive model and the EDA outputs to support proactive HR actions.
